We build a bag that ages with you, for decades — and that only works because of what happens to the leather itself along the way. The short version: yes, full-grain leather gets better with age. The color deepens, the surface softens, and the grain develops a depth that a brand-new bag simply doesn't have. That's called patina, and it's the reason full-grain leather outlasts mass-market bag brands built from corrected or bonded hide, which don't patina — they just wear out.

## Does leather get better with age?

For full-grain leather, yes. Patina is the leather responding to light, oils from your hands, and the friction of daily use — the surface darkens gradually, especially at contact points like the handle, the strap, and the corners where your grip lands most often. It isn't a coating wearing thin; it's the natural grain of the hide reacting to being carried. A bag that's been used for years looks *more* finished than the day you bought it, not less — which is the opposite of how most goods age.

## Does leather patina reduce durability?

No — patina is a surface change, not structural wear. Full-grain hide keeps its original fiber structure intact, because the surface was never sanded down to hide scars and grain the way corrected-grain leather is. That dense fiber structure is what actually determines durability, and patina doesn't touch it. What you're seeing darken and soften is the top layer of the grain; underneath, the hide is doing exactly what it was built to do. The corners round instead of cracking, and the leather stays supple rather than stiffening — signs of a material aging well, not one breaking down.

## Why cheaper leather doesn't patina the same way

Most bags sold at scale use corrected-grain or bonded leather — hide that's been buffed, sanded, or reconstituted so every sheet looks identical off the shelf. That uniform surface can't develop real patina, because there's no original grain left to react to light and use. Instead it just degrades: cracking, peeling, flaking at the stress points. It's the difference between a material that improves with wear and one that's simply wearing down from day one.
